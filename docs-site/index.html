SkinGuard AI

Offline melanoma screening for smartphones. Quantifying and closing the dermoscopy→clinical domain gap.

The Domain Gap: AUC Performance

Chance (0.50)
Melanoma head on dermoscopy 0.9395
Same model on smartphone photos 0.4941
After domain adaptation (Cancer head) 0.7778

Dermoscopy-trained features do not transfer to smartphone photographs. An AUC of 0.4941 is a coin flip. This is why the system ships two heads.

Architecture & Method

The system operates entirely on-device, processing images through a strict sequential cascade designed to reject invalid data before any diagnostic inference occurs.

1. Deterministic Quality Check

Laplacian variance ≥ 12 and brightness 35–225. Heavily blurred or extremely dark/blown-out images are rejected instantly.

2. Out-of-Distribution (OOD) Gate

A MobileNetV3-Small classifier ensures the image contains a valid skin lesion. Rejection occurs before classification. An image blocked by the OOD gate never reaches either diagnostic head and therefore never receives a risk score.

Verification: A textured wall blocked at 1.9% skin detected.

3. Dual-Head Classification

An EfficientNet-B2 backbone with two separate heads. The melanoma head is the headline diagnostic number (despite unproven smartphone performance), while the cancer head is the statistically supported claim for phone photos.

Six findings, including three failures

Reporting what was tried and rejected is what makes the accepted choices credible.

INT8 Quantisation Fails

Evaluated and rejected: EfficientNet's squeeze-excitation blocks are unsupported by dynamic quantisation, producing a model 0.16 AUC worse and 10× slower.

ABCD Features Do Not Transfer

Classical hand-crafted features were evaluated and found not to transfer across imaging domains (AUC 0.691 on dermoscopy vs 0.488 on smartphone).

Catastrophic Forgetting

Clinical fine-tuning came at a measurable cost of a 0.056 AUC drop on the primary dermoscopy task, necessitating separate deployed heads.

Train/Test Leakage is Pervasive

1,293 duplicates spanned both partitions across the public datasets. Removing them changed a memorisation-inflated result into a defensible one.

Clinical Sharpness Validated

Standard photography sharpness thresholds destroy dermoscopy datasets. A Laplacian variance ≥ 50 rejects ~50% of valid images. The validated value is 12.

Exceptional Calibration

Isotonic Regression reduced Expected Calibration Error from 0.2235 to 0.0072 — a 31-fold improvement. A displayed 77% corresponds to an observed rate of 78%.

Limitations

Melanoma head is dermoscopy-validated. Smartphone performance is unproven; the cancer head is the supported claim for phone photos.

Cancer head is a stratifier, not a calibrated probability (Brier 0.19, fitted on only 447 images).

Fitzpatrick types V and VI are represented by 10 and 1 images respectively. Performance on darker skin cannot be claimed — a genuine gap for a Pakistan deployment.

Wound rejection trained on synthetic images only. Real wound photographs were not available.

CAM peak localisation 68.4%. Roughly 1 in 3 overlays may be off-centre.

PAD-UFES-20 contains only 47 melanoma images after QC — the reason the clinical task was reframed as cancer vs non-cancer.
