01 — THE FINDING

Dermoscopy models do not work on phone photos

A dermoscope presses against the skin with polarised light and roughly ten times magnification. A phone camera does none of that. The distributions are different enough that a model trained on one performs at chance on the other — and almost nobody measures it.

Melanoma head on dermoscopy 0.9395
2,832 held-out images · the domain it was trained on
Same model on smartphone photos 0.4941
no adaptation · 0.50 is a coin flip
After domain adaptation 0.7778
2,147 clinical images · cancer vs non-cancer
The centre line marks 0.50 — random guessing. An unadapted dermoscopy model lands almost exactly on it. This is why the system ships two heads: the melanoma head is the primary output but is validated only on dermoscopy; the cancer head is the statistically supported claim for phone photographs.
02 — METHOD

Four stages, and any of them can stop the chain

Rejection happens before classification. An image blocked by the gate never reaches either diagnostic head, so it never receives a risk score at all.

00

Quality check

Laplacian variance and exposure, computed on-device in about 20 ms. Rejection names the failing metric rather than showing a generic error.

≥ 12 · 35–225
01

Out-of-distribution gate

MobileNetV3-Small classifies valid lesion, wound, or not-a-lesion. Verified on device: a textured wall that passed every quality check was blocked at 1.9% skin detected.

99.41% · ≥ 0.85
02

Melanoma head

EfficientNet-B2 with GeM pooling and squeeze-excitation. Produces the primary probability plus a forward-only class activation map.

AUC 0.9395
03

Cancer head

The same backbone, domain-adapted on smartphone images. Covers basal and squamous cell carcinoma as well as melanoma.

AUC 0.7778

Calibration

Temperature scaling followed by isotonic regression. Both stages are required — the decision thresholds were tuned on the isotonic output.

# two stages, both mandatory
p = sigmoid(logit / temperature)      # temperature scaling
p = interpolate(p, isotonic_curve)    # isotonic regression

04 — WHAT WE LEARNED

Six findings, including three failures

Reporting what did not work is what makes the rest credible.

01

Train/test leakage is pervasive and rarely checked

1,293 images appeared in both partitions. Removing them converted a memorisation-inflated result into a defensible one. Perceptual-hash deduplication also removed 3,594 near-duplicates — Bhavesh and ISIC draw from the same archive under different filenames.

02

Standard sharpness thresholds destroy dermoscopy datasets

Laplacian variance ≥ 50, the photography default, rejects roughly half of all dermoscopic images. A lesion is a smooth region on smooth skin, and immersion fluid removes surface texture. The validated value is 12.

03

Hand-crafted ABCD features do not transfer across domains

AUC 0.691 on dermoscopy, 0.488 on smartphone — with border scores saturating at 7.34 out of 8 for both classes. Classical dermatology criteria are domain-specific in a way not previously quantified.

04

Catastrophic forgetting is measurable and consequential

Fine-tuning on clinical images cost 0.056 AUC on the dermoscopy task, from 0.9395 to 0.8837. This is why each head deploys as a separate checkpoint rather than one shared model.

05

INT8 quantisation fails on EfficientNet

Squeeze-excitation blocks and SiLU activations are unsupported by dynamic quantisation. The runtime inserted dequantise–float–requantise wrappers around every unsupported node: 0.16 AUC worse and 10× slower. Shipped fp32.

06

The domain gap can be measured and largely closed

AUC 0.494 → 0.778 on smartphone images through staged adaptation: backbone adaptation on all 2,147 clinical images, then head recalibration. The starting point being exactly chance is the cleanest possible demonstration that the features did not transfer.

05 — LIMITATIONS

What this system cannot do

Stated here and inside the app itself, not buried in a settings screen.

Smartphone melanoma performance is unproven

The melanoma head is validated on dermoscopy only. For phone photographs the cancer head is the statistically supported output, and the interface says so beneath the primary figure.

Dark skin performance is unvalidated

Fitzpatrick types V and VI are represented by 10 and 1 images respectively. Performance on darker skin cannot be claimed — a genuine gap for a Pakistan deployment, where most users are types IV–V.

The cancer head is a stratifier, not a probability

Brier score 0.19, fitted on 447 images. Treat the number as a band rather than a precise figure.

Wound rejection uses synthetic training data

Real wound photographs were unavailable. Synthetic inflamed-tissue images were generated instead; validation on genuine wounds is required before any clinical use.

Heatmap localisation is 68.4%

Roughly one overlay in three may be off-centre. Presented as a region of interest with soft blur, never as a lesion boundary.

Macro non-lesion textures are untested

Out-of-distribution negatives were object and scene photography at normal viewing distance. Macro textures with pigmented-lesion colour palettes — corroded metal, bark, mineral surfaces — were not represented.

Roughly six GPU-hours on a single T4

Datasets carry licences permitting research use but not redistribution, so acquisition is scripted rather than bundled.
